Best Cat Feeders for Two Cats: Shared Schedules vs Microchip Access

Start with the conflict you need to solve. A scheduled hopper can fix late shared meals; a selective-access bowl is the better tool when one cat steals food, grazes, or needs a separate diet.

The short answer

Choose the PETLIBRO Automatic Cat Feeder when both cats eat the same dry food without crowding and the problem is meal timing. Choose the SureFeed Microchip Pet Feeder MPF001 when one cat needs protected access to a manually filled wet- or dry-food bowl. Choose the Closer Pets MiBowl CP500 when a persistent food thief makes an enclosed back and sides the deciding constraint.

Do not buy a shared hopper to solve food stealing. Do not buy a microchip bowl expecting scheduled dispensing. These are different tools, and many two-cat homes need two feeding stations rather than one device.

Manufacturer claims describe intended product behavior, not a guarantee for every cat or microchip. Check implanted-chip compatibility, allow a gradual training period, and keep a manual feeding fallback while changing routines.

One feeder or two?

One shared scheduled feeder can work when both cats already eat the same food without crowding. If one cat grazes or needs a separate diet, protect that cat's bowl and place the other station far enough away that both cats can eat without being trapped in a corner.

Two selective-access bowls offer the clearest separation but also double battery checks, cleaning, training, and floor space. Start with the bowl that is actually being stolen from, then watch the routine before adding more hardware.

Five checks before ordering

  1. Confirm whether the real failure is late meals, food theft, different diets, or wet-food handling.
  2. Verify the exact ASIN and model on Amazon; do not substitute a connected or collar-only variant without rechecking its constraints.
  3. Check the implanted microchip standard and location, or decide whether the cat will tolerate the included collar tag.
  4. Choose a station with open escape paths so one cat cannot corner the other.
  5. Plan bowl washing, battery checks, and a gradual lid-training period before relying on the new setup.
